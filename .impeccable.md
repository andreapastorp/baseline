## Design Context

### Users
Internal development team using the tool during sprint planning ceremonies. Not a public product — no need to onboard strangers. The people using it already know what planning poker is. They want the ceremony to feel focused and efficient, not beginner-friendly.

### Brand Personality
**Three words:** Sharp. Precise. Different.

The interface should feel like a well-calibrated instrument — every element deliberate, nothing decorative that doesn't earn its place. It should be immediately recognizable as *not* the generic dev-tool aesthetic. The goal is quiet confidence, not flash.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Direction: Editorial-industrial.** Think a well-typeset internal report, or a Bloomberg terminal that got a typography budget. Not a card game, not a productivity app — something more like a voting booth or a technical briefing room. The ritual of the reveal (hidden → shown) should feel significant, not playful.

- **Anti-references**: Dark navy + blue accent dashboards (current state), glassmorphism, rounded-corner card grids, anything that looks like a Tailwind starter template.
- **References to draw from**: Swiss grid design, instrument panels, financial data terminals, academic poster layouts.
- **Theme**: Light mode. Planning poker sessions often happen on shared screens in meeting rooms. A light, high-contrast interface projects better and breaks from the conventional dev-tool dark mode. The precision and sharpness reads more clearly on light.
- **Accent color**: Something unexpected — not blue, not green. Consider a deep amber, a muted terracotta, or an ink black with a single precise accent.

### Design Principles
1. **Reduce to essentials.** Every element must justify its presence. If it doesn't help the team estimate faster or more clearly, it's gone.
2. **The reveal is the moment.** The transition from hidden votes to revealed votes is the emotional peak of every round. Design should make this feel weighty.
3. **Numbers first.** This is a quantitative tool. Points, vote counts, spreads — give numbers room to breathe and hierarchy to dominate.
4. **Ritual over playfulness.** Sprint ceremonies are habits. The interface should feel like a reliable tool the team reaches for, not an app that tries to entertain them.
5. **Different by omission.** The distinctive quality comes from what's *not* there — no rounded-corner card grids, no glows, no gradients, no badge clusters. Restraint is the aesthetic.
